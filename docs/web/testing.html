Help testing Pickelhaube

A small favour with a big effect: keep a test version of the app on your Android phone for two weeks.

Why a test?

Google Play has a rule for new developer accounts of private persons: before an app may be published for everyone, it has to go through a closed test. At least 12 testers must have joined it and stay in it for 14 days in a row. Only after that can I ask Google to release the app.

So this is not about finding hidden bugs (though I'm glad about every report) – it is mainly about reaching that number. If a tester drops out before the end, the 14 days start over. That is why I'm looking for a few more than 12.

Who can help

You need

An Android phone (Android 8.0 or newer) with the Google Play Store, and a Google account.

Ideal

A FRITZ!Box with a DECT headset or speakerphone attached – a device that can't dial by itself. That's what the app is made for.

Also fine

A FRITZ!Box with an ordinary telephone attached. Everything can be tested just the same.

No FRITZ!Box?

You can still help: install it, open it now and then, look around the settings. Every tester counts.

A headset or speakerphone is not required. With a normal telephone the app works exactly the same – but since the handset can dial by itself, the benefit is harder to see: it comes down to dialling from your smartphone's contacts and recent calls. Picture the handset as a headset without a keypad, and the point of the app becomes clear. If you have several FRITZ!Boxes (home and office, say), try configuring more than one. When the app makes sense

What to expect

EffortAbout 5 minutes to join, then opening the app now and then
Duration14 days from the day the test reaches 12 testers – plus Google's review afterwards (usually up to about a week). Please stay in until I tell you it's done.
CostNone. The app is free, has no ads and no in-app purchases.
UpdatesTest versions arrive through the Play Store like normal app updates.
FeedbackWelcome, never required. By email, as private feedback in the Play Store, or as an issue on GitHub.
AfterwardsKeep the app if you like it – when it goes public you simply continue with the regular version. Or uninstall it.

How to join

  1. Send me your Google account address Email pickelhaube@rustygnome.org with the address of the Google account you use in the Play Store on your Android phone (usually a Gmail address). Google only lets people on the tester list join. I'll reply once you're added.
  2. Accept the invitation Open the test link while signed in with that Google account – on the phone or on a computer – and tap Become a tester:
    https://play.google.com/apps/testing/org.rustygnome.pickelhaube
  3. Install the app Follow the link to Google Play on that page, or search for Pickelhaube in the Play Store on your phone (it may take a few minutes after accepting until it shows up). Install it like any other app.
  4. Keep it for 14 days Leave it installed and stay in the test. Open it every few days – Google looks at whether testers actually use the app, so a short look now and then really helps. If you have a FRITZ!Box: set it up following the guide and make a call or two.
Ideas for what to try (only if you have a FRITZ!Box): set up the connection · dial a number · pick one from the phone books or recent calls · set a speed dial · add a quick settings tile · switch the app's language. If something is unclear or doesn't work, that is exactly the feedback I'm after.

Your data during the test

  • I see the email addresses on the tester list (you sent yours to me), and Google's general statistics for the test, such as installs and crash reports. I don't see what you do in the app.
  • Google handles the test like any Play Store installation, under its own privacy policy.
  • The app treats test and regular versions the same – see the privacy policy.
  • I'll use your address only to organise this test and delete it from my list once the app is public.

Questions

Do I need a FRITZ!Box?

No. Without one the app can't place calls, but it installs and opens, and you count as a tester. With one, you can test it properly.

Is it safe to give the app my FRITZ!Box password?

The password is stored encrypted on your phone (Android Keystore) and sent only to your box – even over plain HTTP it never travels in clear text. If you prefer, create a separate FRITZ!Box user just for the app, with telephony permission only.

Can I use an iPhone?

Unfortunately not – Pickelhaube is Android only.

What if I have to leave early?

Please let me know, so I can find someone else in time. Leaving: open the test link again and choose Leave the program, then uninstall the app.

Will my phone or my FRITZ!Box be changed?

The app changes nothing on your phone beyond its own data. On the box it only changes what you ask it to – which phone rings for dial help, speed dials, switching a call forwarding, copying a contact into a phone book.

I already joined but can't find the app in the Play Store.

Make sure the Play Store on your phone uses the same Google account you sent me, and that you tapped Become a tester on the test link. Then give it a few minutes, or open the Play Store link from the test page directly.

Ready?

Send a short email – one line with your Google account address is enough. Thank you!
